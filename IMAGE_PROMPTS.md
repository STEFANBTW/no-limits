# No Limits Furniture — Editorial Image Generation & Photography Guide

This comprehensive reference document outlines the visual language, camera angles, shot varieties, and prompt directives required for generating the complete catalog of luxury architectural estate pieces across all 8 Dedicated Estate Spaces.

---

## 1. Master Photography Angles & Shot Taxonomy

Every piece in the No Limits collection requires **5 distinct perspectives** that collectively reveal its sculptural form, structural integrity, material craftsmanship, and spatial presence. When generating or photographing images, follow this 5-shot taxonomy:

### Shot 1: The Hero 3/4 Perspective (Sculptural Presence)
* **Camera Angle**: Eye-level or slightly low (15° elevation), positioned at a 30°–45° diagonal from the front plane.
* **Focal Length**: 50mm–85mm prime lens to avoid wide-angle barrel distortion while preserving natural optical depth.
* **Lighting**: Soft, directional architectural daylight entering from a single large aperture or window. Gentle key light with soft shadow falloff revealing dimensional contours.
* **Objective**: Captures the complete three-dimensional silhouette, proportion, and weight of the furniture piece within a quiet, uncluttered architectural corner.

### Shot 2: The Monolithic Frontal Elevation (Orthogonal Architectural Study)
* **Camera Angle**: Pure eye-level frontal elevation (0° tilt, 0° pan).
* **Focal Length**: 70mm–90mm.
* **Lighting**: Balanced, diffused gallery illumination creating symmetrical subtle gradient shading under horizontal spans, cantilevers, and legs.
* **Objective**: Emphasizes pure geometry, architectural symmetry, horizontal balance, and the stance of the piece.

### Shot 3: The Macro Tactile Close-Up (Material & Joinery)
* **Camera Angle**: Tight oblique macro shot (45° angle, 20–40 cm distance).
* **Focal Length**: 90mm–120mm macro lens, wide open (f/2.0 – f/2.8) for an ultra-shallow depth of field.
* **Lighting**: Raking side-light (grazing light) that accentuates the micro-textures: the pores in Roman travertine, the hand-waxed grain of smoked oak, saddle stitch thread tension, hammered bronze patinas, or fluted stone ridges.
* **Objective**: Provides incontrovertible proof of master craft, tactile authenticity, and material luxury.

### Shot 4: The In-Situ Spatial Context (Atmospheric Estate Staging)
* **Camera Angle**: Wide environmental perspective (35mm–50mm) captured at seated eye level (approx. 1.1m from floor).
* **Focal Length**: 35mm–50mm.
* **Lighting**: Atmospheric, moody ambient light native to the specific estate space (e.g. golden hour light raking across limestone floors, warm 2700K recessed glow in a wine cellar, or diffused hydrothermal vapor in a wellness suite).
* **Objective**: Shows the furniture piece fully anchored in an architect-designed private estate environment, interacting with bespoke rugs, textured plaster walls, and natural landscape apertures.

### Shot 5: The Planar Overhead / 45° Bird's-Eye (Volumetric Silhouette)
* **Camera Angle**: Elevated 45° to 60° high-angle, looking down upon the upper surfaces, seats, or tabletop planes.
* **Focal Length**: 50mm–70mm.
* **Lighting**: Soft overhead ambient wash with subtle ground-plane cast shadows.
* **Objective**: Reveals surface grain flow, book-matched marble veining, top-grain leather quilting, ergonomic seat contours, and spatial footprints.

## 5. Verification & Consistency Checklist for Image Generations

Before approving an image batch:
1. **Object Consistency**: Ensure the piece retains identical materials, proportions, joinery, and color across all 5 camera angles.
2. **Context Integrity**: Verify the backdrop matches the space's designated palette and architectural tone without distracting clutter.
3. **No Phantom Artifacts**: Check that legs, seams, and handles adhere strictly to real-world structural physics and joinery.
4. **Lighting Purity**: Avoid synthetic multi-colored neon or artificial digital glows; all lighting should read as genuine architectural or solar light.
